### 1. What a neutrino is

| Property | Value / Description | How we know it |
|----------|--------------------|----------------|
| **Fundamental particle** | Lepton of the Standard Model (SM) | First detected in nuclear β‑decay (Cowan & Reines, 1956) |
| **Electric charge** | 0 e (electrically neutral) | No electromagnetic scattering observed; limits <10⁻²¹ e |
| **Spin** | ½ (fermion) | Measured from angular distributions in weak decays |
| **Mass** | Non‑zero but tiny – ≲ 0.1 eV (upper limits) | Oscillation experiments give Δm² ≈ 10⁻⁵–10⁻³ eV² → lower bound ≈ 0.008 eV; KATRIN β‑decay limit mνₑ < 0.8 eV (90 % CL) |
| **Interaction** | Only the weak force (and gravity) | Cross‑sections ≈10⁻⁴⁴ cm² at MeV energies; confirmed by scattering on nuclei/electrons |
| **Lifetime** | Effectively stable (τ ≫ age of Universe) | No decay observed; solar‑neutrino flux stable over decades |

### 2. Flavours and mass eigenstates

* **Flavour eigenstates** – νₑ, ν_μ, ν_τ. They are produced/detected together with the corresponding charged lepton (e, μ, τ).
* **Mass eigenstates** – ν₁, ν₂, ν₃ with definite masses *m₁, m₂, m₃*. The relation is

\[
\nu_\alpha = \sum_{i=1}^{3} U_{\alpha i}\,\nu_i ,
\]

where *U* is the **PMNS matrix** (three mixing angles θ₁₂, θ₂₃, θ₁₃, one Dirac CP‑phase δ_CP, and possibly two Majorana phases).

* **Mass ordering** – either **normal** (m₁ < m₂ < m₃) or **inverted** (m₃ < m₁ < m₂). Current global fits favour normal ordering at ~3 σ.

* **Dirac vs. Majorana** – Open question: are neutrinos distinct from antineutrinos (Dirac) or identical (Majorana)? Tested by neutrinoless double‑β decay (0νββ). No signal yet; limits on the effective Majorana mass ≲ 0.1 eV.

* **Sterile neutrinos** – Hypothetical singlet states (νₛ) that do not couple to the weak force. Short‑baseline anomalies (LSND, MiniBooNE) hinted at eV‑scale sterile ν, but recent SBN data place strong constraints; the issue remains unsettled.

### 3. Why neutrinos matter

| Domain | Role of neutrinos |
|--------|-------------------|
| **Standard Model** | First confirmed particle interacting only via the weak force; their masses require physics beyond the minimal SM (e.g., Yukawa couplings, seesaw mechanisms). |
| **Flavor physics** | Neutrino oscillations are the only observed lepton‑flavor violation, providing a laboratory for CP violation in the lepton sector. |
| **Astrophysics** | Core‑collapse supernovae emit ≈ 99 % of their binding energy as neutrinos; detection of SN 1987A neutrinos confirmed theoretical models. |
| **Cosmology** | Relic neutrinos affect the radiation density (N_eff) and suppress small‑scale structure; cosmological data bound the sum of masses Σ m_ν ≲ 0.12 eV. |
| **High‑energy astrophysics** | IceCube and similar detectors see PeV‑scale neutrinos, giving a unique view of the most energetic cosmic accelerators. |
| **Dark‑matter searches** | Solar and atmospheric neutrinos constitute the “neutrino floor”, an irreducible background for direct‑detection experiments. |

### 4. Recent experimental highlights (≈ 2020‑2024)

| Experiment / Observation | Key result | Why it matters |
|---------------------------|------------|----------------|
| **T2K (Japan)** | > 3 σ indication of **δ_CP ≈ ‑π/2** (maximal CP violation) | First strong hint that the lepton sector may violate CP symmetry. |
| **NOvA (USA)** | Preference for **normal mass ordering** and θ₂₃ > 45° (upper octant) | Reinforces T2K hints; combined fits increase ordering significance. |
| **JUNO (under construction, commissioning 2024‑25)** | Will measure reactor ν̅ₑ spectrum with sub‑% precision to resolve the mass ordering. |
| **IceCube‑Gen2 (proposal)** | Continued detection of a diffuse > 100 TeV astrophysical neutrino flux; identification of a few point sources (e.g., TXS 0506+056). | Confirms neutrinos as messengers of extreme astrophysical processes. |
| **KATRIN (Germany)** | Direct β‑decay limit **m_νₑ < 0.8 eV** (90 % CL); aim for 0.2 eV by ~2025. | Improves model‑independent absolute‑mass bound. |
| **LEGEND‑200 / nEXO (0νββ searches)** | No signal; half‑life limits > 10²⁶ yr → **|m_ββ| ≲ 0.07 eV**. | Tightens the parameter space for Majorana neutrinos. |
| **SBN program (MicroBooNE, SBND, ICARUS)** | No confirmation of LSND/MiniBooNE low‑energy excess; strong constraints on eV‑scale sterile ν. | Reduces support for simple 3 + 1 sterile models. |
| **Planck + BAO + DESI (cosmology)** | Updated bound **Σ m_ν < 0.12 eV** (95 % CL). | Consistent with oscillation lower bound; limits degenerate‑mass scenarios. |

### 5. Core assumptions underlying current knowledge

| Assumption | Where it enters | How it is tested |
|------------|----------------|------------------|
| **Three‑flavour oscillation framework** | All global fits of solar, atmospheric, reactor, and accelerator data | Consistency across disparate experiments; any deviation would hint at sterile states or non‑standard interactions. |
| **Unitarity of the PMNS matrix** | Extraction of mixing angles and CP phase | Precision short‑baseline experiments look for missing probability (non‑unitarity). |
| **Standard V–A weak interaction** | Cross‑section calculations for detection | Measured neutrino‑electron and neutrino‑nucleus scattering agree with V–A predictions; deviations would signal new physics. |
| **ΛCDM cosmology** | Translating CMB/large‑scale‑structure data into Σ m_ν limits | Alternative cosmologies (extra relativistic species, early dark energy) could modify the bound. |
| **Neutrinos are stable** | Flux stability over time (solar, supernova) | No decay signatures observed; limits on τ/m > 10⁹ s/eV. |

---

### 6. Limitations of present data

| Limitation | Consequence |
|------------|-------------|
| **Absolute mass scale** | Oscillations give only Δm²; β‑decay, 0νββ, and cosmology provide complementary but model‑dependent limits. |
| **Mass ordering** | Preference at ~3 σ, not yet the 5 σ discovery needed for a definitive claim. |
| **CP‑violating phase δ_CP** | Current hints are statistically limited; a robust measurement awaits DUNE and Hyper‑K. |
| **Majorana nature** | No observation of 0νββ; nuclear‑matrix‑element uncertainties make interpretation of limits non‑trivial. |
| **Sterile neutrino existence** | Conflicting experimental results; conclusive answer requires higher‑statistics short‑baseline programs. |
| **Neutrino‑nucleus interaction modeling** | Nuclear effects (final‑state interactions, multinucleon processes) introduce systematic errors in energy reconstruction, limiting precision on mixing parameters. |

---

### 7. Open questions & near‑future frontiers

1. **Absolute mass scale** – pursued by KATRIN, Project 8, and upcoming cosmological surveys (CMB‑S4, Euclid).
2. **Mass ordering** – to be resolved by JUNO, DUNE, Hyper‑K, and atmospheric detectors (PINGU, ORCA).
3. **Leptonic CP violation** – DUNE and Hyper‑K aim for a 5 σ discovery if δ_CP ≈ ‑π/2.
4. **Dirac vs. Majorana** – next‑generation ton‑scale 0νββ experiments (LEGEND‑1000, nEXO, CUPID) will probe the inverted‑ordering region.
5. **Sterile neutrinos / non‑standard interactions** – SBN, IsoDAR, COHERENT, and precision solar neutrino measurements will test these possibilities.
6. **High‑energy astrophysical sources** – IceCube‑Gen2, KM3NeT, and radio‑based detectors (ARA, ARIANNA) aim to identify point sources and measure flavor ratios.
7. **Cosmological impact** – refined measurements of N_eff and Σ m_ν will test for extra radiation or lepton asymmetry in the early Universe.

---

### 8. Bottom line

Neutrinos are neutral, spin‑½ fermions that interact only via the weak force (and gravity). The three known flavours (νₑ, ν_μ, ν_τ) are quantum superpositions of three mass eigenstates, giving rise to **neutrino oscillations**—the first experimental evidence that neutrinos have non‑zero mass. Their tiny masses, mixing, and the unresolved question of whether they are Dirac or Majorana particles place them at the frontier of particle physics, astrophysics, and cosmology.

Recent experiments have sharpened our knowledge of the mixing angles, provided the first credible hint of CP violation in the lepton sector, and opened a new window on the high‑energy Universe with astrophysical neutrinos. Yet fundamental issues—absolute mass, ordering, CP phase, and the Majorana nature—remain open. A coordinated global program of reactor, accelerator, underground, and cosmological experiments scheduled for the 2020s and beyond is designed to answer these questions.

**Assumptions**: the three‑flavour, unitary PMNS framework; standard V–A weak interactions; ΛCDM cosmology; neutrino stability.
**Limitations**: current statistical precision, nuclear‑physics uncertainties, and dependence of cosmological mass limits on the assumed cosmological model.

Understanding neutrinos fully will not only complete the picture of the Standard Model but also shed light on the origin of mass, the matter‑antimatter asymmetry, and the dynamics of the most energetic phenomena in the cosmos.
